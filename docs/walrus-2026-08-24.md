## Decisions

- Appellate design: merits re-adjudication — the dispute expanded with the appeal ground is re-ruled under the ruleset in force; affirm iff the merits action matches the contested action; one instance, affirm exhausts due process. Rationale: pure function of content; no new non-determinism; no infinite regress; the institution exists independent of legislative quality
- Appeal construction from transcript: dispute = first-argument statements of the first two parties in order of appearance; contested ruling = ruling on the appellant's last argument statement; ground = settlement text. Rationale: deterministic and principled, not tuned to polity-001 — which falls out of it anyway
- Final settlement law: ACCEPT settles; APPEAL settles only if affirmed; REJECT unsettles. Rationale: the corrected settlement assumption made executable
- Repairs over ceremony: COE proposed; owner directed "just fix the errors in RAROC priority" — instances repaired, gate extension declared as deviation instead of adopted as document
- Test-structure deviation re-declared: original sunset condition (kernel modules ≤6) lapsed at 7 modules; accepted while `test_determinism.py` covers only cross-module properties and every module with its own contract has a dedicated test file
- v2 design: stance expressed as ordered content markers (refusal/contestation before general match) — deterministic, no LLM at the ruling seam; v1 frozen as legislative history, CLI default stays v1, v2 by explicit invocation — legislative versioning is explicit
- Res judicata across law change: the v1 settlement stands; the v2 re-hearing is the legislative-evolution demonstration, not a retroactive undoing — institutional stability over retroactive consistency
- Merits/procedure separation: merits re-adjudicate the original dispute; the appeal ground is provenance (content-addressed in the digest), not merits input. Rationale: merits that consume the ground are gameable — the outcome must depend on the dispute and the law in force, not the rhetoric of the appeal; neutrality extended from parties to pleadings
